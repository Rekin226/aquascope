## [1.1.0] - 2026-10-28

The map computes. Click a river and route a flood wave down it, flood its valley in your
browser and draw a levee, replay a past flood as a story, or draw a storm, a reservoir, a well or a
land-use change and watch the answer recompute as you drag. Each model is checked against a
published example or reference data, and each works from the Explorer, the CLI, the MCP server and Ask the
map. All of it is experimental (docs/stability.md); nothing stable changed.

### Added
- **Route a flood: a flood wave down GEOGLOWS's own river network, on the globe** (#656, part of #664). `aquascope.routing` (experimental) routes water with Muskingum reach by reach over GEOGLOWS v2's own topology, k (seconds) and x, read from its public routing configuration, at GEOGLOWS's hourly step; each reach takes the sub-reaches and sub-steps that keep every coefficient non-negative, as river-route does. `route` sends a flood (a triangle) or a release (a block) of a set peak, or the reach's T-year flow, down the path to the sea and returns per reach the added peak, the hour of the peak, the wave's front and the attenuation, with the gauges and towns on the way; `route_storm` turns a storm drawn as a polygon into runoff with a plain runoff coefficient and routes it down every river below. Checked against GEOGLOWS's own hourly retrospective on the Elbe, Rhine, Danube and Mississippi: NSE 0.89 to 0.99, the peak within 6 % and its timing within 5 h (no routing: NSE -0.31 to 0.73); `aquascope route check` repeats it. In the Explorer, **Route a flood** on a river's map card plays the wave on the river lines with the time bar, with the peak and its hour on the towns and gauges downstream, a card for each and the run in the action log; Ask the map reads "route a flood of 500 m3/s from Basel" and "release 800 m3/s for 2 days from here" into the new `route` action without a model. `aquascope route flood|storm|check` and the MCP tools `route_flood` and `route_storm` are the same engine. See [docs/explorer.md](docs/explorer.md#route-a-flood).
- **Paint the globe: one question at every gauge in view, with field significance** (#657, part of #664). `aquascope.paint` (experimental) answers one question per gauge from the Archive and tests the pattern as a whole: the trend in annual floods (annual maximum daily flow over complete hydrological years, October to September north and April to March south, a year complete with 330 of its days) and in low flows (annual minimum 7-day mean over climate years), each as the Mann-Kendall p-value and Sen's slope in % of the mean per decade, at least 20 years by default; and the baseflow index from `signatures.parquet`. Field significance is the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate at twice the global level (Wilks, 2016). Every gauge in the box gets a status (painted, too short, not in the Archive, over the cap, unreadable), and the result carries the licences of the sources painted, a CSV table and a methods paragraph with its references. The questions are one registry, so the next ones (100-year flood per km², GEOGLOWS against the gauge) plug in. In the Explorer, the brush in the map tools paints the view: the Archive files are read ten at a time and answered in the worker in batches, so the dots fill in as they land (300 gauges over England and Wales in about 35 seconds headless), with a diverging key, significant and not significant marked, the unpainted gauges shown small and grey, a card per dot, **Read 300 more**, and a PNG figure, the CSV and the methods from the legend. The map action `paint` (Ask the map: "where are floods getting bigger in Europe?") runs it and goes in the undo log. `aquascope paint` and the MCP tool `paint_map` are the same function. Checked against the package's single-station trend on five Archive gauges (same p-values and years at full precision) and, qualitatively, against Blöschl et al. (2019) for north-western Europe. See [docs/paint.md](docs/paint.md).
- **Flood it: a screening flood of a river's valley from a click, on the visitor's GPU** (#658, part of #664). Experimental. On a reach's map card, **Flood it** floods the valley at the reach's 100-year flow and draws it on the globe as it spreads. `aquascope.floodsim` plans the grid: the main stem up and down GEOGLOWS's own network, the window cut to the valley, every reach drawn there burnt in (moved onto the valley floor, joined to the reach below, four-connected) to the depth that carries its own 2-year flow, and an inflow for every river entering or starting in the grid at the same return period as the clicked reach (Log-Pearson III on GEOGLOWS's annual maxima, read for all reaches at once from `retrospective/maximums.zarr`), with walls and a normal-depth outfall where the river leaves. The local-inertial scheme (Bates et al. 2010; de Almeida et al. 2012) runs to a near steady state (wet area and volume each under 1% change an hour, capped at 72 h) on WebGPU, or in a worker on cells twice as large, with frames drawn as they come in the flood views' depth colours. The panel docked at the side picks the flow (2- to 100-year, the forecast peak, a number, the 15-day forecast or Route it's wave as a hydrograph) with its m³/s and source, and opens the run in the flood views (hazard, arrival, a section, what gets flooded) as a UGRID file; two runs compare with a swipe. `aquascope flood` writes the depth as GeoTIFF and the run as UGRID NetCDF; the MCP tool `flood_reach` and Ask the map's `flood_reach` action ("flood Upton upon Severn at the 100-year flow") are the same engine, and every run is in the undo log. The numpy, JS and WGSL schemes match two fixtures step for step; checked against JRC's 100-year depth on the Severn with the Avon (CSI 0.715), the Thames (0.453), the Ahr (0.431) and the Cedar River in Iowa (0.651), and shallower by a median 0.5 m to 2.8 m, mostly because GEOGLOWS's 100-year flows are 1.5 to 3.4 times GloFAS's there. See [docs/floodsim.md](docs/floodsim.md).
- **Levees and breaches: draw one on Flood it's valley and run again** (#663, part of #664). Experimental. `aquascope.floodsim` gains `apply_levee` (every cell a drawn line crosses, by a supercover line so a diagonal levee has no corner gap, raised to a crest given as an elevation in m above mean sea level, not a height above the ground), `apply_breach` (lowered to an invert or back to the natural ground), `edit_grid` (levees, then breaches, on a copy; the same lines always give the same cells), `levee_profile` (the ground and the flood along a line and a crest 0.5 m above the flood), `compare_runs` and `compare_exposure` (buildings, roads and people under two runs, as two steps of one UGRID file through the flood views' own count). In the Explorer, **Draw a levee** and **Draw a breach** under a run draw a line on the map; a crest control shows the ground, the flood and the crest along it; **Run again** puts the new run beside the old one in the swipe, and **What gets flooded, before and after** counts the change inside the box where the runs differ, with the buildings kept dry and newly flooded on the map. Lines stay on the map, can be moved, re-set and removed, and each change is in the undo log; phone width works. `aquascope flood --levee FILE --crest M --breach FILE --invert M`, the same arguments of the MCP tool `flood_reach` (a run before and after, and the change) and Ask the map's `draw_levee` and `draw_breach` ("draw a levee 1 m above the flood", "breach the levee") are the same engine. Checked: a levee higher than the flood keeps every cell behind it at exactly 0 m, straight or at an angle; one lower than the water overtops with the volume budget closed to 5e-16; a breach passes 0.76 to 1.07 times a broad-crested weir's flow at heads of 0.84 m to 1.69 m on Flood it's 24 m cells (the scheme has no critical-flow control, so the match depends on the head over the cell size; the sweep is documented); the JS twin matches numpy on a fixture with a diagonal levee and a breach to 1 µm and the WGSL to 22 µm, with the same steps. The Upton upon Severn example is an illustrative ring round the town, not the real defence's alignment or crest. See [docs/floodsim.md](docs/floodsim.md#levees-and-breaches).
- **Replay an event: a past flood as a story on the map** (#659, part of #664). Experimental. `aquascope.replay` assembles a flood from its data, each piece with its source, licence and coverage, or saying plainly that it is not available: IMERG V07's daily rain averaged over the upstream basin (through SERVIR's ClimateSERV), the Archive's gauges on the river against GEOGLOWS's retrospective at each gauge's reach (the observed and modelled peak, the error and the timing), what GEOGLOWS's ensemble issued 1, 3 and 7 days before the peak said for the peak day (read from GEOGLOWS's forecast archive on S3, runs from July 2024), the observed flow routed downstream with Route it (`aquascope.routing.route` now takes a real hydrograph, `inflow_cms`), and Flood it at the peak with the buildings, roads and people under it. `story` writes it as version 1 of a small story format (stops with a camera, a date, the layers and a note filled by code from templates) that other parts of AquaScope can write too; `report` writes a Markdown report; `candidates` lists Floods past's floods near a place in a month. In the Explorer, **Replay** in Floods past's cards and on a reach's or a gauge's card assembles the flood in the worker and plays it: the camera flies between the stops, the time bar and the layers follow, the note sits small at the bottom (a sheet on a phone) with a small chart, Flood it runs on the GPU at the peak, and play, pause, step and a scrubber drive it; it exports a GIF, a WebM video and the report, and a `#rp=` link replays it. `aquascope replay`, the MCP tools `replay_candidates` and `replay_event`, and Ask the map's `replay` action ("replay the Severn flood of November 2024", the code picking the flood from the candidates it lists) are the same engine, and every replay is in the undo log. Two events replayed end to end and checked against their sources: the Severn at Saxons Lode in Storm Bert (433 m³/s on 26 November 2024, the Environment Agency's own daily mean; GEOGLOWS 112 m³/s) and the French Broad at Asheville in Hurricane Helene (1,950 m³/s on 27 September 2024, USGS's daily value 68,900 ft³/s; GEOGLOWS 2,041 m³/s); the forecast numbers match an independent xarray read of the same runs to the hundredth, and the basin areas match the agencies' catchment areas within 0.5 %. See [docs/replay.md](docs/replay.md).
- **Drop a well: the drawdown cone on the map, Theis with superposition** (#660, part of #664). Experimental. **Drop a well** in the Explorer's sketchpad menu drops a pumping well where you click, and its drawdown cone is drawn round it as contour lines (5 cm, the cone's edge, dashed, then 10 cm, 20 cm, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 m ...) over a fill; more wells add, a well dragged takes its cone with it as you drag, a well's rate, the transmissivity, the storativity and the days recompute it, and **Play the cone growing** plays it on the time bar. A well's card gives its drawdown at its face, its own share and the others', and over your MODFLOW model the cell, its size, the layer and what the model's well cell would read (the Theis drawdown at Peaceman's radius, 0.2 times the cell's side). Every change is in the undo log; on a phone the panel is a bottom sheet. `aquascope.sketch.drawdown` computes it through `aquascope.groundwater.aquifer`'s well function; the defaults (1,000 m³/day, T 500 m²/day, S 1e-4, 30 days) carry their typical ranges from Heath (1983, USGS WSP 2220). Checked against Fetter (2001)'s Theis worked example (printed 5.7 m, computed 5.73 m) and the 32 values of W(u) in USGS Professional Paper 708, Table 5 (all within 1e-4), with superposition's symmetry and reciprocity. `aquascope well`, the MCP tool `drop_well` and Ask the map's `drop_well` action ("drop a well at Lyon pumping 2000 m3/day") are the same function. See [docs/sketchpad.md](docs/sketchpad.md).
- **Draw a storm: rain on an area you draw, routed down every river below on the globe** (#670, part of #664). The Explorer's map tools gain the sketchpad, a menu of tools that draw on the map and compute on it; its first is **Draw a storm**: draw an area (tap its corners or drag a box, with the levee tool's drawing), set the rain in mm, its hours and the runoff coefficient (a plain share of the rain that runs off, 0.3 by default, said as such on the card), and **Route the storm** runs `aquascope.routing.route_storm` in the worker. The wave plays on the rivers on the time bar through Route it's own layers; the card (a sheet at the bottom on a phone) has the hydrographs of the reach that gets the most and of the main outlet, the runoff and the share of it that has left by the outlets. Dragging a corner routes the storm again. The area and each run are in the action log, each undoable. `route_storm` now also returns those hydrographs, the box of the reaches reached and the mass balance (`out_m3`, `out_pct`). Ask the map reads "50 mm of rain over this area in 6 hours" into the new `draw_storm` action without a model (the area on the map, one the reader draws, a polygon, a box or a place). Checked: over mid-Wales 50 mm with 0.3 over 1,457.6 km² of unit catchments is 21.9 million m³ of runoff, and the outlets pass all of it in 185 h. See [docs/sketchpad.md](docs/sketchpad.md).
- **Change land use: a drawn area's runoff and peak flow before and after, SCS curve number** (#661, part of #664). Experimental. **Change land use** in the Explorer's sketchpad menu draws an area, picks what it becomes (TR-55's covers: residential lots, commercial, industrial, paved, newly graded, open space, row crops, pasture, meadow, woods, or a share of impervious area) and a 24-hour design storm, and computes in the browser: the cover now from ESA WorldCover 2021 (the Explorer's land-cover layer, CC BY 4.0), the hydrologic soil group from HYSOGs250m (Ross et al. 2018; the Natural Capital Alliance's CC BY 4.0 cloud-optimised copy, read by range requests) or the one picked, the slope from the Terrarium terrain, TR-55's curve numbers, runoff and graphical peak discharge with Tc from NEH 630's lag equation, before and after. The area is filled in what it becomes with its peak's change, the GEOGLOWS reach it drains to is lit, the panel gives the numbers, the cover bar, the sources and their licences, and the added peak can be routed downstream with Route it; a corner dragged recomputes, and every change is in the undo log. `aquascope.sketch.landuse` checks against TR-55's examples 2-1 to 2-4 (CN 70, 75, 77, 74; runoff 2.81, 3.28, 3.48 in, and 3.18 where the book prints 3.19) and 4-1 (345 cfs), and NEH 630's lag example (1.14 h). `aquascope landuse`, the MCP tool `change_landuse` and Ask the map's `change_landuse` ("turn this field into a car park with 80 mm of rain") are the same function. See [docs/sketchpad.md](docs/sketchpad.md#change-land-use).
- **Draw a reservoir: a lake on a river, and the flood it flattens on the globe** (#662, part of #664). Experimental. `aquascope.sketch` is the sketchpad's package, and `aquascope.sketch.reservoir` routes a flood through a reservoir with level-pool (storage-indication) routing: `level_pool` solves 2S2/dt + O2 = I1 + I2 + 2S1/dt - O1 each step on the working curve; `plan` finds the river under a drawn lake (the GEOGLOWS reaches drawn inside it) and the dam at the exit of the river through it; `stage_storage` turns the Terrarium terrain inside the lake into its stage-area and stage-storage curves; `spillway` is a broad-crested weir; `route_reservoir` runs Route it with the dam's reach passing through the reservoir (`aquascope.routing`'s `route`, `route_storm` and `route_network` take `through`), so the outflow goes on down the river, and runs it without the reservoir too. The flood is the T-year flow at the dam, a peak, a Route it flood from upstream or a drawn storm (#670). In the Explorer, **Draw a reservoir** in the sketchpad draws the lake, reads its terrain from the tiles in the page, and after **Route it through** the wave past the dam is the flattened one on the time bar; the card has the inflow and outflow hydrographs at the dam, the peak reduction and delay, the highest water and whether it overtops the dam, and the peak furthest down with and without it. Dragging a corner plans and routes again; the lake and every run are in the action log. `aquascope route reservoir` (`--check` prints the check), the MCP tool `route_reservoir` and Ask the map's `draw_reservoir` ("draw a reservoir with a 40 m spillway at 12 m and route the 100-year flood through it") are the same engine. Checked against USDA NRCS, National Engineering Handbook Part 630, Chapter 17 (Amend. 69, April 2014), Example 17-2: Table 17-12's outflow is reproduced within 0.51 ft3/s, the peak 364 ft3/s on day 5.3 as in the book. See [docs/sketchpad.md](docs/sketchpad.md#draw-a-reservoir).

### Changed
- **One design flow in every tool** (part of #664). Route it and Draw a reservoir read a reach's T-year flow as Flood it does, Log-Pearson III on GEOGLOWS v2's annual maxima of simulated daily flow (`retrospective/maximums.zarr`, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), instead of a GEV by L-moments on the daily record, so the same reach and T give the same m³/s in Flood it, Route it, the reservoir and Replay. `aquascope.floodsim.design_flow` (with `reach_curve` and `flow_words`) is the shared function, and the Explorer's `design-flow-core.js` reads the curve and words the method and source the same way on every card; Route it's card now names its flow. The return period of `route`, `route_reservoir`, their CLI and MCP faces and Ask the map's `route` is now 2 to 500 years, the range the curve is fitted over. On the Severn above Newtown the 100-year flow is 65 m³/s (64.6; it was 64 by the GEV).

### Fixed
- **Phone layout of the sketchpad and Flood it** (part of #664). Change land use's result is now a short bottom sheet like the storm's and the reservoir's (it was a top sheet over most of the screen), and the camera keeps the drawn area above it; the reservoir's Dam top value lines up with the other two, and its camera keeps the lake and its corners above the sheet, so a corner can be dragged at phone width; Flood it's sheet sits above the time bar.
- **Flood it's labels** (part of #664). A flow typed in m³/s is said once ("954 m³/s at this reach", not "954 m³/s (954 m³/s)"), also in `aquascope flood`'s output. A steady run's time bar counts hours since the water was turned on ("5 h into a steady run", "steady after 27 h") instead of reading like a clock time. The warning that the valley is wider than the grid now says what it means and what to do, sits under the result instead of in a fold, and appears only when the water stands against a wall away from the rivers (`floodsim.wall_contact`, the same rule in the Explorer; it was raised by a single wall cell with 5 cm of water, even beside the river's outlet).

A Floods past event's river is the main river near it, the largest upstream area within 25 km at its reach nearest the event's centre (`replay.main_river`), so Storm Bert's reports west of the Severn no longer replay a tributary (a Strahler order 5 stream 11.9 km away) instead of the Severn. The Floods past card is kept in the map's view as its events and Replay buttons arrive (it could open past the top edge). The share link now carries a Floods past event's reports, so a fresh page replays the same story (its first note differed). Checked headless: the WebM export (MediaRecorder, VP9) and a shared link opened on a fresh page.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-27

AquaScope 1.0. The globe is where the work happens: click a river, a gauge or your own model
result and see it on the map, with studies, floods ahead and past, and an AI that acts on the
map and can be undone. 1.0 is also a promise: [docs/stability.md](docs/stability.md) names the
public Python, CLI, MCP and Archive surface, and from here a breaking change waits for 2.0 with
a deprecation of at least six months. Everything marked experimental there can still move.

### Added
- **The Archive can be deposited on Zenodo, for a dataset DOI of its own** (part of #444 and the 1.0 milestone). `aquascope.archive.zenodo` (internal) builds a deposit bundle from the dataset at one Hugging Face commit: the catalogue, `signatures.parquet`, `health.json`, the dataset card, the `obs/` JSON files and one `.tar` per variable and source (only redistributable sources; any other is refused; catalogue and signature rows of sources whose licence is unknown are left out until their terms are verified, and named), with `SOURCES.md` (every source's licence and attribution) and `MANIFEST.json` (sizes, SHA-256, snapshot date). The metadata is a dataset with the catalogued and mirrored counts as two numbers, the record licence Other (Attribution) with each source's own licence listed, and a supplement to the software DOI; derived folders stay on Hugging Face. `deposit` makes a new record or a new version of a concept record through Zenodo's bucket API and leaves a draft unless asked to publish. [`archive-zenodo.yml`](.github/workflows/archive-zenodo.yml) runs it by hand (sandbox first, a draft by default, secrets `ZENODO_SANDBOX_TOKEN` and `ZENODO_TOKEN`) and writes nothing to Hugging Face. No DOI exists yet; [Citing the Archive](docs/archive.md#citing-the-archive) says how the maintainer makes the first deposit.
- **MODFLOW results that look like a modeller's tools: layers in 3D, cross-sections, hydrographs and the water budget** (#625, #626, part of #627). Experimental, in `aquascope.groundwater.layers` and `aquascope.groundwater.budget`; the plan view and the stable surface are unchanged.
  - `read_layer_elevations` reads each layer's top and bottom (and `IDOMAIN`) from MODFLOW 6 `.dis`/`.disv`/`.dis.grb` and MODFLOW-2005 `.dis` (a confining bed left as a gap between its layers), matching FloPy on the Freyberg example; `model_layers` puts them on the drawn cells with a vertical exaggeration for 3D; `cross_section` cuts a drawn line through every layer (cells in order with their edges to half a metre, tops, bottoms, every layer's head at every time, the water table); `cell_hydrograph` gives a cell's head over time in every layer.
  - `read_budget` and `water_budget` read the cell-by-cell budget file (`.cbc`) in the full, compact (IMETH 1 to 5) and MODFLOW 6 (IMETH 6) layouts, single or double precision, with numpy only, and give each term's in and out per saved time and MODFLOW's percent discrepancy (Harbaugh, 2005, USGS TM 6-A16); flows between cells are left out. Tested on synthetic files of every layout with known terms and against FloPy's `CellBudgetFile`.
  - In the Explorer, on **Your MODFLOW heads**: **3D layers** (each layer between its top and bottom, coloured by head, the exaggeration said and changeable, layers hideable or pulled apart, **Plan** to go back), **Cross-section** (draw a line; a dock shows the layers, the saturated ground, the water table and deeper heads, stepping with the time bar, with the drawing's exaggeration and a close look around the heads), a cell's card with its head over the run in every layer, and **Water budget** when a `.cbc` is dropped with the other files (in and out by term over time, the current time's table and discrepancy).
  - `aquascope modflow` gains `--layers`, `--section LON,LAT ...`, `--cell ROW COL` and `--budget CBC`; the MCP tool `modflow_heads` gains `section`, `cell_row`, `cell_col` and `budget_path`.
  - `tests/test_groundwater/synthetic_models.py` writes a synthetic three-layer model with a pumping well and its compact budget (`modflow-layered/`), and budget files in every layout for the tests.
- **MODFLOW heads read like a hydrogeologist's tools** (#623, #624, part of #627). `aquascope.groundwater.views` (experimental) turns one step and layer of a structured grid into labelled head contours at one interval for the whole run, flow arrows down the head gradient (Darcy's law, the same conductivity in every direction), drawdown from a reference step, wells (from a dropped `.wel` file, MODFLOW-2005 or 6, or found where the drawdown peaks), pathlines toward the pumping wells and each well's capture zone from backward tracking, all within the layer and with the step's heads held still (no conductivity, porosity or vertical flow is in a head file: streamlines, not travel times). In the Explorer, **Your MODFLOW heads** gains **Head**, **Drawdown** and **Capture**, drawn over a smooth head surface and following the layer and the time bar, and a well card; the Freyberg sample now fetches its `.wel` (327 kB, same pinned FloPy commit). `aquascope modflow` gains `--views`, `--wel` and `--reference`. Tested on an analytic plane (straight pathlines, evenly spaced contours, on a rotated grid too) and on the synthetic pumping well (found at row 15, column 23, its cone centred there, the backward capture zone matching forward tracking). See [docs/modflow.md](docs/modflow.md#contours-flow-drawdown-and-capture-zones).
- **What 1.0 promises** (#588, part of the 1.0 milestone). The public surface is now written down and tested.
  - [`docs/stability.md`](docs/stability.md): the four public surfaces (Python names, CLI commands and flags, MCP tools and arguments, the Archive's paths and columns), what counts as breaking and what does not (a corrected number is not), how a deprecated name keeps working and warns (`AquaScopeDeprecationWarning`, a `FutureWarning`; a stderr line on the CLI; a `"deprecated"` key in an MCP result) until the next major release and for at least six months, and what experimental means.
  - [`docs/archive-layout.md`](docs/archive-layout.md): every Archive prefix (the catalogue, `obs/`, `basins/`, `skill/`, `forecasts/`, `forecasts/warnings/`, `feeds/`, `flowstatus/`, `scout/`, `waterbalance/`, `bulletins/`, `context/`, `health/`) with its files, columns, units, cadence, licence and the workflow that writes it, checked against the live dataset on 2026-10-12 (file listing, Parquet schemas, manifests).
  - `aquascope.experimental` lists what is shipped but not frozen: everything first released in 0.26.0 and 0.27.0 (rivers, now and next, evidence, watch, bulletin, context, engineering exports, the `map_*` modules, flood depth, flow against normal, the water balance, mesh review, what if on the map, MODFLOW heads, SWMM) and `aquascope.gym` (the benchmark, now AquaScope Bench, #440). 17 CLI verbs and 36 MCP tools are experimental; 30 CLI verbs (44 commands with their subcommands) and 40 MCP tools are stable.
  - `aquascope._deprecation`: the `DEPRECATED` register and the `moved` (module `__getattr__`) and `deprecated` (function) shims. Empty at this release: nothing is deprecated yet.
  - `tests/test_public_api.py` and the snapshot `tests/fixtures/public_api.json` (458 Python names in 48 modules, the stable CLI commands with their flags, the stable MCP tools with their arguments): CI fails when a stable name, command, flag, tool or argument disappears without a deprecation, when a deprecated name stops warning, when a stable MCP tool gains a required argument, when a new command or tool is not classified, and when a published Archive prefix is missing from the layout page. `python tests/test_public_api.py --update` adds new stable names and never removes.
- **Flood results: a section, a hydrograph, and what gets flooded** (#621, #622, part of #627). Both modules are experimental.
  - `aquascope.flood_section`: `section(mesh, line)` gives the ground and the water level along a line at every model step, with the highest water of the run and the widest and deepest water along it; `hydrograph(mesh, cell)` gives the depth and the water level at one cell over the run with the arrival, the peak, the recession and the duration as HEC-RAS Mapper defines them (a threshold depth, a second peak kept in one duration). `MeshIndex` finds the cell under many points at once.

## [0.21.0] - 2026-09-30

### Added
- **Studies go past the stationary design flow.** Seven new study steps in `aquascope.advanced`, each a plain JSON tool that the Explorer, `aquascope studio`, the Analyst and `aquascope mcp` all run:
  - `change_points`: Pettitt, PELT and Mann-Kendall with Sen's slope on the annual maxima or means. It says whether the record is one sample.
  - `nonstationary_flood`: a GEV whose location moves with time, set beside the stationary GEV, with the likelihood-ratio test, AIC and a bootstrap interval.

  - `climate_projection`: change factors from seven CMIP6 HighResMIP models through the Open-Meteo Climate API, run through the calibrated GR4J for the change in mean flow, low flow and the flood. The spread across models is reported, not only the median.

- **Three new playbooks for the questions that come after the design flow.** `flood_change` asks whether the flood is getting worse. `climate_change` asks how the river changes by 2050. `catchment_response` asks what happens if the rain drops or it warms. - **The Studio asks what the study needs, one question at a time, as a pick list.** A playbook can now carry a `checklist`: the things the study must know before it plans, each with the question, one line on what the answer changes, the options and the words in a question that already answer it (`aquascope.playbooks.ChecklistItem`, `checklist_open`). A brief that asks more than one thing (two question clauses or a comparison cue) and names another playbook's intent outright plans the companion playbooks too (`playbooks.companions`, the `COMPANIONS` table) and appends the steps the primary plan lacks: a duplicate tool or method is left out, a helper step follows the step it fed, a quantity the primary plan derives (the crop step's peak demand) counts as given for the companion's rules, and the plan block says what was added and why; the brief records every playbook it asks for (`Brief.kinds`). On the 25 plan-quality cases the tree goes from 0.97 to 1.00 and off-tree from 0.73 to 1.00 with nothing extraneous, which retires the one asterisk on the keyless promise.
- **The validator refuses an invalid choice instead of swapping it** (#413). An argument with a closed set of values used to be replaced by the first allowed value with a note; it is rejected with the choices named, so the Methodologist's repair round fixes it, and the workbench tools declare their sets (wqi use and variant, baseflow method, return_periods distribution, irrigation method) next to the Analyst tools that had them. A fallback is validated under its step's id, so a step whose only fault is its fallback keeps its place and loses the fallback: the recorded Kingston cross-check had reached `regionalize_signatures` with `method: regionalization` and died at run time.
- **Accuracy plumbing** (#414). Every Author draft, keyed or not, passes the Critic's sentence check (a sentence whose numbers or years are in no result is dropped and counted in the report and the footer; a paying user used to get less enforcement than the on-device model); a failed deterministic check is a fix for the Author with the repair each check asks for, keyless the template repair drops the refused sentences; the second critique after the fix round keeps the model; a report whose critique is still not ok opens with one line naming the failed checks (`report["notice"]`, `critique_ok` in the reply payload); p-values below 0.001 print as such; a price table per model id, `cost_usd` per role in the ledger, a `max_usd` ceiling on `Studio`, `--max-usd` on the CLI and `max_usd` on the MCP tools that switch the roles to keyless behaviour once reached, all in the footer.

### Fixed
- **Multi-word catalog searches** (#306). `find_stations(query="Kingston Thames")` returned nothing: the whole string was one substring tested against the name alone. Several words now match across name, id and river, accent-folded and case-insensitive ("Thames at Kingston", "Rhône Anthon"), with a ranked fallback to the best partial matches; single-word queries behave as before.
- **Answer checks misread ranges and labels** (#324). The number check no longer reads the dash of a range as a minus sign ("297-348" gave -348) nor glues a label's digits onto the next number ("Q2 325" gave 2325); real negatives such as skew = -0.864 still parse. The Analyst is also told not to add site facts from memory unless labelled "from general knowledge, not from the data", and a new `years_traceable` check lists any year in the answer that no tool result contains.
- **`flood_analysis()` reduces daily input to annual maxima for Gumbel and GEV L-moments** (#333). `aquascope.api.flood_analysis()` documents `discharge` as a daily or sub-daily series, but `method="gumbel"` and `method="gev_lmoments"` passed it straight to `fit_gumbel()` / `fit_gev_lmoments()`, whose first parameter is `annual_maxima` and which treat every value as one. A six-year daily record was therefore fitted as ~2,200 annual maxima, so the return levels described the distribution of daily flows rather than of annual floods (a nominal Q100 of 90.6 against 125.8 once fitted correctly). Both methods now resample to annual maxima the way `gev` and `lp3` already did, so the quantity fitted no longer depends on which distribution is chosen; input without a `DatetimeIndex` is still passed through untouched, since it is already annual maxima. `gpd` is unaffected — it takes peaks over a threshold by design.
- Taiwan data.gov.tw collection now uses the WRA-hosted UUID datasets with strict TLS verification, preserves legacy numeric dataset IDs, and returns the complete response unless a client requests a limit.
- **GEMStat collector validates Zenodo payload before caching** (#172). When Zenodo returned an HTML interstitial, error, or non-zip stream, `fetch_raw` previously wrote the body directly into `data/cache/gemstat_<hash>.zip`, causing `zipfile.BadZipFile` on every subsequent fetch due to persistent cache poisoning. `fetch_raw` now resolves the download URL from `links.content` or `links.self`, streams to a `.tmp` file, validates that the `Content-Type` is not HTML and the initial bytes start with `PK` zip magic bytes, and cleans up temporary or corrupt cache files on failure so non-zip payloads raise diagnostic errors instead of unhandled exceptions.
- **Format p-values consistently across CLI and dashboard** (#303). `cli.py` and `dashboard/views/extremes.py` previously formatted p-values with `:.4f` and `:.3f`, allowing non-zero values (e.g. `p = 1e-12`) to print as `0.0000` or `0.000` and appear as exactly zero. Added a shared `format_p_value` helper in `aquascope.utils.formatting` following the Explorer's convention (`"< 0.001"` floor, three decimals otherwise, and `"—"` for non-finite/null values) and updated both call sites.
- **TLS verification failures against `iot.wra.gov.tw` and `opendata.wra.gov.tw`** (#169). Both hosts chain to a TWCA-issued certificate missing the Subject Key Identifier extension, which Python 3.13+ rejects under its default strict profile (`SSL: CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED, Missing Subject Key Identifier`). `taiwan_wra_iot.py` and the `WRA_BASE` clients in `taiwan_wra.py` (water level, reservoir, groundwater, well-metadata) now use `relax_strict_tls=True` — already established for `codis.cwa.gov.tw` (#177) — which drops only that strict-profile check while keeping full chain and hostname verification on; this also replaces `verify=False`, which had been disabling verification outright. `taiwan_wra_iot.py` is also rewired to the v2 API's actual `groundwaterlevel/stations` endpoint and response shape (the old guessed v1-style paths all 404); rainfall is no longer offered for this source since the v2 API gates it behind paid membership with no free endpoint.
- **GEV bootstrap discard tracking and Mann-Kendall p-value formatting** (#272): `FloodFreqResult` now exposes `n_bootstrap` and `n_bootstrap_discarded` counts when bootstrap confidence intervals are computed, surfacing when parameter bounds ($|c| \le 0.50$) discard resamples. The counts are threaded through `aquascope.explore`, `aquascope.workbench`, and the MCP server. In the Explorer, the Floods panel renders a footer caveat when $\ge 5\%$ of resamples are discarded, and Mann-Kendall trend p-values are formatted with `fmtP` (reporting `< 0.001` instead of `p = 0` for tiny p-values).
- **WQP no longer downloads the whole state** (#170). `/Result/search` returns CSV with no server-side row cap, so a state query buffered hundreds of MB (317 MB / 452 s / 836k rows) before `max_results` was applied. `fetch_raw` now streams the response off the underlying httpx transport and stops reading once `max_results` rows are seen, so a query downloads only the window it needs.
- **WQP failures are loud instead of silent empties** (#170). The old collector caught every error and returned `[]`, so a slow or dead endpoint looked like a genuine "no data" response. Errors now log descriptively and re-raise, and the client uses a payload-appropriate 600 second timeout; now, only a genuine empty body returns `[]`. Where streaming transport is unavailable, it warns that streaming is off and falls back to a buffered request.

A new `CachedHTTPClient.get_text()` method backs the WQP CSV path.
- **Taiwan WRA water level** (`collectors/taiwan_wra.py`): readings now carry station coordinates when the feed provides them (TWD97/WGS84 lat-lon keys) instead of always setting `location=None`.
- **USGS API key** (`collectors/usgs.py`): the collector no longer hard-defaults to the rate-limited `DEMO_KEY`. It reads `api_key=...` or the `USGS_API_KEY` environment variable, and warns before falling back to `DEMO_KEY`.
